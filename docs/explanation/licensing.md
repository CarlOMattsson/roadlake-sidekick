## What the license allows

- You may use Sidekick free of charge, with every feature unlocked, for your own private use, for non-commercial use and for your own internal business purposes.
  It does not matter if Sidekick helps you or your employer earn money.
- You may change the code for the same purposes.
- You may share an unchanged or changed copy with others only free of charge and for non-commercial purposes, or inside your own company for its internal business.
  Nobody may charge for copies, for access, or for keys, and only the licensor may sell license keys.
- You may pay someone else, such as a consultant or an agency, to install, integrate, adapt, change, deploy and keep running Sidekick for you on your own installation.
  They may charge for that work, but not for the software, for keys or for access, and they may not offer Sidekick to you as a hosted service.
  What they deliver must keep the key check and the reminder, and a key in your installation must be your own.
- Every copy, including a private one and a copy you give to someone else, must keep the license key check working and keep the reminder described below visible
  where Sidekick shows its status. You may not remove or hide the reminder or get around the check. The license does not prescribe exactly how the reminder looks.
  A copy you hand to others, also to a colleague or over your company's network, must not contain a key that is not valid for them.
- If you change the code, say so in the copy, and keep the license text and the copyright notices.

## The license key

The key is optional. No right under the license depends on it.

- Without a key Sidekick shows a small reminder that this is the free version. In the MCP apps it is a small coin at the bottom of each room, with the words "Gratisversion" (Free version) next to it, and at the end of an inline card's open row.
  In the Android Today widget it is a small coin in the header of the widget; tapping it opens the app.
- A valid key removes only that reminder. Nothing else changes and no feature is locked.
- With a key, Settings (Android app) and Control (MCP apps) show "Licensed to" and the name on the key, and the day it runs out.
- A key is time-limited. When it expires only the reminder comes back. Your data and features stay as they were.
- A key is signed by the licensor and checked offline against a public key, so Sidekick never contacts a server to check it.
  Only the licensor can make valid keys.
